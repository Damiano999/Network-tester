How quality is rated

Excellent: ping <40 ms, jitter <15 ms, download >25 Mbps.

Very good: if not Excellent, ping <60 ms, jitter <25 ms, download >15 Mbps.

Good: if not rated above, ping <100 ms and download >5 Mbps.

Fair: if not rated above, ping <150 ms and download >2 Mbps.

Limited: ping ≥150 ms or download ≤2 Mbps.

Ping and jitter reflect responsiveness and stability; download reflects capacity.

Test settings

Choose an endpoint using the selector beside the Start test button. Latency, download and upload are measured against that server. Tests may consume data; availability can vary.

Background runs use a Web Worker. Your browser may still pause work in a hidden tab; keep this page open for best results.

Privacy policy

Last updated: 18 October 2026

Network test is a static web app. It does not ask you to create an account. This notice explains what the app stores and what happens when you use each feature.

Information stored in your browser

Your selected settings and up to 20 recent results are kept in this browser’s local storage. Results may include the time, selected endpoint, test duration, ping, jitter, download speed, upload speed and number of rounds. This data is not sent to a Network test account or server. It remains on this device until you clear the site’s browser storage. Anyone with access to this browser profile may be able to see it.

Requests to other services

  • Public IP lookup: the app contacts ipify to display your public IP address. The service necessarily receives the request from your network and may process its IP address and request metadata under its own policy.
  • Connection tests: ping, download and upload requests go directly from your browser to the selected endpoint. The endpoint operator can receive your public IP address, request metadata and the traffic needed for the test. The app uses endpoints operated by the provider named in the selector; each provider sets its own retention and privacy practices.
  • Traceroute: the target, probe location and protocol are sent to Globalping to run a remote measurement. The trace runs from its probe, not from your device. Globalping receives the request and may process it under its policy.
  • Device location: location lookup is off by default. If you enable it in Settings, your browser will ask for permission. The app displays the coordinates and accuracy supplied by the browser and does not send those coordinates to the test endpoint or its own server.
  • PDF export: the report is generated in your browser using the results already stored there. Choosing to print or save it may store or share the report through your browser or operating system.

Hosting and tracking

The site is hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub and the services above may process technical request data under their own policies. The app does not add analytics or advertising trackers. Third-party services can change their practices independently of this app.

Your choices

You can avoid IP lookup and traceroute by not using those controls, keep location lookup off, stop a running test, and clear this site’s local storage through your browser settings. Connection tests require sending requests to the selected endpoint.

Last updated: 18 October 2026

Use Network test for ordinary troubleshooting of connections and systems you are permitted to diagnose.

  • Use authorized targets: run connection tests for your own connection. Only enter traceroute targets that you own or have permission to diagnose.
  • Use public services responsibly: test endpoints and Globalping probes are operated by third parties. Follow their published terms and do not interfere with, overload, probe for vulnerabilities, or disrupt their services.
  • Be mindful of data and cost: tests transfer data, especially with longer durations and larger profiles. Check your plan before running a test on a metered or constrained connection. Stop the test if you no longer want it to continue.
  • Understand the results: measurements depend on your device, browser, network conditions, route and endpoint. They are estimates for troubleshooting, not a guarantee of service quality, a formal audit, or security advice.
  • Availability: third-party endpoints and remote probes may be unavailable, rate-limited or changed by their operators. A failed measurement does not by itself prove that your internet connection is down.

By using the app, you agree to use it lawfully and follow these rules. If you do not agree, do not run a test.
